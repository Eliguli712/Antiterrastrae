01

Volumetric imaging
& biomechanics

DURF-supported research · Working reports

A Scanco micro-CT workflow for an Otodus megalodon tooth: 3,050 DICOM slices, more than 20 GB, and a surface reconstruction of approximately 33 million triangles.

Methods & contribution
  • Segmentation, marching cubes, Taubin smoothing, geometry repair and tetrahedral meshing.
  • Finite-element material and geometric sensitivity analysis using COMSOL.
  • Confirmed z-step: 18 μm. XY pixel spacing and voxel isotropy require separate calibration.
  • Transferable methods: spatial calibration, imaging quality checks and numerical verification.

02 / A question to explore

When two senses disagree,
which cue carries more weight?

I want to understand how reliability shapes multisensory perception. This small model makes that question tangible.

Try a cue-weighting model.

Two noisy observations estimate the same quantity. Change the uncertainty of each cue or introduce a conflict between them. The combined estimate shifts toward the more reliable observation.

The model uses inverse-variance weighting under independent Gaussian noise and a shared-cause assumption. These assumptions make a useful baseline whose failures could be tested experimentally.

Illustrative model with synthetic inputs. This demonstration contains no participant measurements or neural recordings.

03 / Future directions

From simulation
to perception research.

Vrontiki Synaisthesma is my long-term research and game-design concept for multisensory interaction and representations of different sensory worlds.

Proposed summer research

Visual–haptic integration

Fit transparent models to a focused behavioral task. Study reliability, cue conflict, bias and individual differences; compare held-out predictions and inspect where the baseline fails.

Long-term research concept

Cross-species sensory worlds

Use acoustic and geometric environments to ask how an observer’s available signals shape interaction. My paleontological interests—especially ancient whales and ichthyosaurs—provide a distinctive source of questions.

Neural-data analysis and controlled behavioral experiments are training goals. My completed imaging work concerns fossil micro-CT, and my current acoustic work is a physical simulation.
